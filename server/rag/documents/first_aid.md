# BloomCare Emergency & Clinical First Aid Protocols

## Burns & Scalds (Thermal & Chemical)
- **First Aid Procedure:**
  1. Immediately cool the burn area under gentle, cool running tap water for at least 10 to 20 minutes.
  2. Carefully remove rings, tight clothing, or jewelry near the burn before tissue swelling starts.
  3. Cover the clean burn area loosely with sterile non-adherent gauze or clean cling film to prevent contamination.
  4. Take paracetamol or ibuprofen for pain management if appropriate.
- **Contraindications (What NOT to do):**
  - NEVER apply ice, ice water, butter, flour, cooking oil, toothpaste, or traditional concoctions (causes tissue hypothermia and severe infection).
  - NEVER pop or rupture blisters (blisters are sterile barriers preventing bacterial entry).
- **When to go to the hospital:**
  - Burns covering large areas (>3% of body surface area or larger than the casualty's palm).
  - Burns on the face, neck, hands, feet, joints, or genitalia.
  - Deep burns with charred, white, or leathery skin, or any electrical or chemical burns.

## Cuts, Lacerations & Bleeding Wounds
- **First Aid Procedure:**
  1. Apply firm, direct pressure over the bleeding wound using a clean sterile pad, cloth, or gloved hand.
  2. Elevate the injured limb above heart level if no fracture is suspected.
  3. Once bleeding slows, gently irrigate the wound with clean drinking water or sterile saline to remove debris.
  4. Apply an antiseptic ointment and secure with a sterile breathable bandage.
- **When to seek emergency care:**
  - Blood is spurting rhythmically or does not stop after 10 minutes of direct, continuous pressure.
  - Deep gaping wounds with fat, muscle, or bone exposed (requires surgical suturing within 6–8 hours).
  - Wounds caused by dirty rusty nails or animal bites (requires tetanus toxoid and rabies evaluation).

## Nosebleeds (Epistaxis)
- **First Aid Procedure:**
  1. Sit upright and lean slightly forward (do NOT tilt the head back, which causes blood to drain down the throat, leading to choking or vomiting).
  2. Pinch the soft, fleshy part of both nostrils firmly between thumb and forefinger for 10 to 15 continuous minutes.
  3. Breathe quietly through the mouth and apply an ice pack wrapped in cloth across the bridge of the nose.
- **When to seek emergency care:**
  - Bleeding continues for more than 20 minutes despite uninterrupted firm pressure.
  - Associated with significant head trauma, high blood pressure crisis, or difficulty breathing.

## Choking (Airway Obstruction)
- **Adult / Child over 1 year:**
  1. Encourage casualty to cough forcefully if they can still speak or breathe.
  2. If the airway is completely obstructed (unable to cough or speak, clutching throat):
     - Give 5 firm back blows between shoulder blades with the heel of the hand while leaning them forward.
     - If unresolved, perform 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich Maneuver): stand behind casualty, wrap arms around waist, place fist thumb-side just above the navel, and pull sharply inward and upward.
     - Repeat cycle of 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts until object is expelled or help arrives.
  3. If casualty becomes unresponsive, lower to ground and commence CPR immediately.
