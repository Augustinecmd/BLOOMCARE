# BloomCare Clinical Guidance: Common Diseases & Symptoms

## Malaria (*Plasmodium falciparum*)
- **What it means:** A protozoan parasitic infection transmitted by the bite of an infected female *Anopheles* mosquito. In Uganda, *Plasmodium falciparum* is the predominant and most dangerous species.
- **Common Symptoms:**
  - High intermittent fever, chills, and shaking rigors
  - Profuse drenching sweats
  - Severe headache, joint stiffness, and generalized body aches
  - Loss of appetite, nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea
  - Severe fatigue, general malaise, and anemia
- **What you can do:**
  - Perform a Malaria Rapid Diagnostic Test (RDT) or blood microscopy smear prior to starting medications.
  - Maintain vigorous hydration with clean water, oral rehydration solutions, or soups.
  - Use paracetamol for fever reduction while awaiting diagnostic results.
  - Sleep under insecticide-treated mosquito nets (ITNs).
- **When to seek immediate medical care:**
  - Inability to retain fluids or oral medications due to persistent vomiting.
  - Confusion, extreme drowsiness, seizures, or loss of consciousness (Cerebral Malaria).
  - Yellowing of eyes/skin (jaundice), tea-colored urine, or extreme pallor (severe anemia).

## Common Cold & Influenza (Flu)
- **What it means:** Acute viral infections of the upper respiratory tract. The common cold is caused primarily by rhinoviruses; influenza is caused by influenza viruses A and B.
- **Common Symptoms:**
  - Nasal congestion, rhinorrhea (runny nose), sneezing
  - Sore or scratchy throat, hoarse voice
  - Dry or productive cough
  - Mild to moderate fever, headaches, and body aches (more pronounced in influenza)
- **What you can do:**
  - Prioritize bed rest and warm oral fluids (lemon, honey, herbal teas).
  - Steam inhalation or saline nasal spray/drops to relieve nasal obstruction.
  - OTC paracetamol or ibuprofen for body aches and fever.
  - Avoid unnecessary antibiotics: viral respiratory infections do NOT respond to antibiotics.
- **When to seek medical care:**
  - Difficulty breathing, wheezing, or chest pain.
  - Symptoms lasting longer than 10–14 days without improvement.
  - Persistent high fever (>38.5°C) that fails to respond to antipyretics.

## Cough (Acute & Persistent)
- **What it means:** A protective reflex clearing secretions and foreign irritants from the airways. Can be dry (irritant/viral) or productive/chesty (with mucus).
- **Common Causes:** Viral respiratory tract infections, allergic bronchitis, asthma, post-nasal drip, gastroesophageal reflux (GERD), or bacterial chest infections.
- **What you can do:**
  - Warm water with honey and lemon (natural demulcent for adults and children over 1 year).
  - Stay well-hydrated to help thin respiratory secretions.
  - For dry cough: soothing demulcent syrups or guaifenesin/dextromethorphan as recommended by a pharmacist.
  - Avoid irritants such as tobacco smoke, dust, and cold dry air.
- **When to seek medical care:**
  - Coughing up blood (hemoptysis) or rust-colored sputum.
  - Shortness of breath, rapid breathing, or stridor.
  - Chronic persistent cough lasting more than 3 weeks (requires tuberculosis screening).

## Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD) & Heartburn
- **What it means:** A chronic digestive condition where stomach acid and contents flow backward into the esophagus, causing mucosal irritation and burning discomfort.
- **Common Symptoms:**
  - Retrosternal burning pain (heartburn) often following fatty or spicy meals or when lying down.
  - Acid regurgitation, sour taste in mouth, water brash.
  - Bloating, belching, nausea, and discomfort in upper abdomen (epigastric area).
- **What you can do:**
  - Eat smaller, more frequent meals; avoid lying flat for at least 2–3 hours after eating.
  - Elevate the head of the bed by 15–20 cm.
  - Identify and reduce dietary triggers (spicy foods, caffeine, alcohol, chocolate, fried foods).
  - Use short-term OTC antacids (aluminum/magnesium hydroxide) or PPIs (omeprazole) under pharmacist guidance.
- **When to seek medical care:**
  - Chest pain that radiates to jaw, neck, or left arm (must rule out cardiac emergency).
  - Difficulty or pain when swallowing (dysphagia).
  - Unexplained weight loss or vomiting blood / black tarry stools.

## Acute Diarrhea & Gastroenteritis
- **What it means:** Frequent passage of loose, watery stools (three or more times per day), frequently caused by viral, bacterial, or protozoan gastrointestinal infections.
- **Common Symptoms:** Watery stool, abdominal cramps, nausea, vomiting, low-grade fever, mild dehydration.
- **What you can do:**
  - **Immediate priority:** Prevent dehydration by administering Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS) solution continuously.
  - Continue breastfeeding and feeding easy-to-digest starchy foods (porridge, bananas, rice).
  - Administer Zinc sulfate daily for 10–14 days for children under 5 years.
  - Wash hands thoroughly with soap and running water before food handling.
- **When to seek medical care:**
  - Visible blood or mucus in stool (dysentery).
  - Signs of severe dehydration: sunken eyes, dry tongue, lethargy, skin pinch goes back very slowly.
  - Persistent diarrhea lasting more than 5–7 days.
