# BloomCare Pharmaceutical Monographs & Medication Safety

## Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)
- **Generic Name:** Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)
- **Drug Class:** Non-opioid Analgesic and Antipyretic
- **Prescription Status:** Over-The-Counter (OTC)
- **Primary Uses:** Relief of mild to moderate pain (headache, toothache, muscular aches, dysmenorrhea) and reduction of fever.
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits prostaglandin synthesis within the central nervous system and blocks pain impulse generation; acts on the hypothalamic heat-regulating center to produce antipyresis.
- **Common Side Effects:** Rare at therapeutic doses. Rarely skin rash, urticaria, or minor gastrointestinal discomfort.
- **Important Precautions & Warnings:**
  - Severe risk of hepatotoxicity (liver toxicity) in cases of overdose or when combined with alcohol.
  - Maximum daily adult dose is 4,000 mg (4 grams) within 24 hours.
  - Check co-administered cold or flu medications to avoid accidental duplicate paracetamol ingestion.
- **Dosage Considerations:** Pediatric dosages strictly require body weight calculation (10–15 mg/kg per dose). Always read product labels or consult a pharmacist.

## Coartem (Artemether / Lumefantrine)
- **Generic Name:** Artemether 20mg / Lumefantrine 120mg
- **Drug Class:** Artemisinin-based Combination Therapy (ACT) Antimalarial
- **Prescription Status:** Prescription Only (Rx)
- **Primary Uses:** First-line treatment of acute, uncomplicated malaria infections caused by *Plasmodium falciparum* in Uganda.
- **Mechanism of Action:** Artemether rapidly clears circulating malaria parasites through endoperoxide cleavage and free-radical generation; lumefantrine clears residual parasites with an extended elimination half-life.
- **Common Side Effects:** Headache, dizziness, anorexia, nausea, abdominal discomfort, palpitations, joint pain.
- **Important Precautions & Warnings:**
  - Mandatory confirmation with Malaria Rapid Diagnostic Test (RDT) or blood smear before initiating therapy.
  - Must be taken with fatty food, whole milk, or a meal to ensure adequate lumefantrine absorption.
  - Complete the full 3-day 6-dose course even if symptoms resolve quickly to prevent drug resistance and recrudescence.

## Amoxicillin
- **Generic Name:** Amoxicillin (as trihydrate)
- **Drug Class:** Beta-lactam / Aminopenicillin Antibiotic
- **Prescription Status:** Prescription Only (Rx)
- **Primary Uses:** Bacterial infections of the ear, nose, throat, respiratory tract (bronchitis, pneumonia), skin, and urinary tract. Ineffective against viral infections (common cold, flu).
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding to penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs), leading to bacterial cell lysis.
- **Common Side Effects:** Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, mild skin rash.
- **Important Precautions & Warnings:**
  - Contraindicated in patients with known penicillin or beta-lactam allergies due to risk of anaphylaxis.
  - Complete the full prescribed course to prevent antimicrobial resistance.
  - Report watery or persistent bloody diarrhea immediately (risk of *C. difficile*).

## Ibuprofen
- **Generic Name:** Ibuprofen
- **Drug Class:** Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drug (NSAID)
- **Prescription Status:** Over-The-Counter (OTC) for lower strengths; Prescription for higher strengths
- **Primary Uses:** Relief of pain, inflammatory symptoms (arthritis, muscular strains, dental pain), and fever reduction.
- **Mechanism of Action:** Reversibly inhibits cyclooxygenase-1 and 2 (COX-1 and COX-2) enzymes, decreasing prostaglandin synthesis.
- **Common Side Effects:** Dyspepsia, gastric irritation, nausea, dizziness, fluid retention.
- **Important Precautions & Warnings:**
  - Always take with or immediately after food to minimize gastrointestinal ulceration and bleeding.
  - Avoid in patients with active peptic ulcers, severe renal impairment, or uncontrolled hypertension.
  - Avoid in third trimester of pregnancy due to premature closure of ductus arteriosus.

## Omeprazole
- **Generic Name:** Omeprazole
- **Drug Class:** Proton Pump Inhibitor (PPI)
- **Prescription Status:** OTC / Prescription depending on pack size and indication
- **Primary Uses:** Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), acid reflux, heartburn, peptic ulcer disease, and stomach protection during NSAID therapy.
- **Mechanism of Action:** Irreversibly inhibits the gastric H+/K+-ATPase enzyme system in gastric parietal cells, significantly suppressing basal and stimulated acid secretion.
- **Common Side Effects:** Headache, abdominal pain, constipation, flatulence, nausea.
- **Important Precautions & Warnings:**
  - Take 30 to 60 minutes before breakfast for optimal acid suppression.
  - Swallow whole; do not crush or chew delayed-release enteric-coated capsules or tablets.
  - Long-term continuous use requires clinical monitoring for magnesium deficiency and bone density.

## Cetirizine
- **Generic Name:** Cetirizine Hydrochloride
- **Drug Class:** Second-Generation Non-Sedating Antihistamine
- **Prescription Status:** Over-The-Counter (OTC)
- **Primary Uses:** Symptomatic relief of allergic rhinitis (sneezing, runny nose, itchy watery eyes), allergic skin conditions, and urticaria (hives).
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitively antagonizes peripheral H1 histamine receptors, reducing histamine-induced vascular permeability and pruritus.
- **Common Side Effects:** Mild somnolence (fatigue), dry mouth, mild headache. Much lower sedation compared to first-generation antihistamines (chlorpheniramine).
- **Important Precautions & Warnings:**
  - Caution when driving or operating machinery if drowsiness occurs.
  - Dose adjustment required in patients with moderate to severe renal impairment.

## Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS) + Zinc Sulfate
- **Generic Name:** Low-Osmolarity Oral Rehydration Salts with Zinc Sulfate
- **Drug Class:** Electrolyte Replenisher & Micronutrient Supplement
- **Prescription Status:** Over-The-Counter (OTC)
- **Primary Uses:** Prevention and treatment of dehydration caused by acute diarrhea or vomiting in children and adults; recommended by WHO/UNICEF.
- **Mechanism of Action:** Sodium-glucose co-transport mechanism promotes water and electrolyte reabsorption across the intestinal brush border. Zinc shortens diarrhea duration, reduces stool volume, and speeds mucosal recovery.
- **Common Side Effects:** Generally well-tolerated. Vomiting may occur if administered too rapidly.
- **Important Precautions & Warnings:**
  - Dissolve exactly 1 ORS sachet in 1 liter of clean drinking water (never mix with juice, milk, or boiling water).
  - Administer in small, frequent sips using a clean cup or spoon.
  - Zinc supplementation should continue for 10 to 14 consecutive days even after diarrhea resolves.
