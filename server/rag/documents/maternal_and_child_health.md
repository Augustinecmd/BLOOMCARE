# BloomCare Maternal, Infant & Child Health Guidance

## Medication Safety During Pregnancy
- **Core Principles:**
  - Nearly all pharmaceutical substances cross the placenta to some degree. Self-medication during pregnancy poses severe risks to fetal organogenesis and development.
  - Paracetamol is generally considered the preferred first-line analgesic/antipyretic in pregnancy when used at the lowest effective therapeutic dose for the shortest necessary duration.
  - Avoid NSAIDs (such as ibuprofen, diclofenac, aspirin) particularly during the third trimester because they can cause premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus and impair fetal renal function.
  - Always consult a doctor or BloomCare clinical pharmacist before taking any prescription or OTC medication, herbal supplement, or traditional remedy during pregnancy.

## Essential Prenatal Micronutrients
- **Folic Acid (Vitamin B9):**
  - Essential to prevent neural tube defects (such as spina bifida and anencephaly).
  - Recommended daily dose: 400 mcg daily, ideally started at least 1 month prior to conception and continued through the first trimester.
- **Iron & Folic Acid Supplementation:**
  - Crucial to treat and prevent maternal iron-deficiency anemia, low birth weight, and preterm labor.
  - Take iron with citrus juice or vitamin C to maximize absorption; avoid taking simultaneously with tea, coffee, or milk.

## Pediatric Dosage & Medication Safety Factors
- **Body Weight-Based Dosing (mg/kg):**
  - Pediatric dosages must ALWAYS be calculated based on the child's exact weight in kilograms (mg/kg), NOT estimated based on age alone.
  - Children have immature hepatic metabolism and renal excretion pathways, making them highly vulnerable to medication toxicities.
- **Accurate Administration Tools:**
  - Always use an oral dosing syringe, calibrated dropper, or measuring medicine cup. Never use household kitchen teaspoons or tablespoons, which vary unpredictably in volume from 2.5 mL to 9 mL.
- **Never Give Aspirin to Children or Adolescents:**
  - Aspirin is strictly contraindicated in children and teenagers recovering from viral infections (cold, flu, chickenpox) due to the risk of Reye's Syndrome, a rare but life-threatening condition causing acute liver damage and cerebral edema.

## Infant Fever & Danger Signs
- **Infant Fever Care:**
  - Dress infant in light, breathable cotton clothing.
  - Offer frequent breastfeeds or fluids to prevent rapid dehydration.
  - Never sponge infants with cold water or alcohol (causes shivering and raises core temperature). Use lukewarm water if necessary.
- **Urgent Pediatric Red Flags:**
  - Any infant under 3 months with a rectal or axillary temperature of 38.0°C (100.4°F) or higher requires immediate emergency hospital evaluation.
  - Lethargy, floppiness, inability to suckle or feed, abnormal high-pitched cry, sunken fontanelle (soft spot), or convulsions.
